Comparative Evaluation of Predictive Machine Learning vs. Volumetric Risk Exposure for Multi-Tenant SEO Content Maintenance¶

Abstract¶

  1. This study addresses the problem of identifying organic search performance drops across multi-tenant website architectures to optimize content maintenance scheduling.
  2. Utilizing the FlyRank dataset containing over 78 million daily performance records, we engineered relative temporal and behavioral signals to train an out-of-sample Random Forest Classifier.
  3. Programmatic validation using a strict GroupShuffleSplit demonstrated that the machine learning model achieved an out-of-sample $F_1\text{-score}$ of 0.7678, failing to outperform a naive "Always Decline" baseline of 0.7780.
  4. Further signal isolation audits revealed that engineered relative changes act as statistical noise, providing negligible class separation.
  5. Consequently, we present an alternative rule-based playbook that sorts assets by absolute exposure volume, successfully capturing major traffic collapses while providing a transparent, technical debt-free prioritization engine for operations.

1. Question¶

The research question and the decision it supports.

Research Question¶

Given a period of historical SEO traffic signals (April and May 2026), can we accurately predict whether a page's organic search visibility will undergo a significant decline ($\le -20\%$ change in impressions) in the subsequent month (June 2026)? Furthermore, if a predictive machine learning model fails to outperform a naive baseline, can we extract actionable risk-exposure metrics to construct a transparent, rule-based content prioritization framework?

Decision Supported¶

This work supports strategic content operations and search engine optimization (SEO) resource allocation. Specifically, it determines whether a content team should deploy an automated, predictive machine learning classifier or rely on a deterministic, volume-based risk-exposure queue to decide which URLs require immediate review, content refreshes, or technical audits.

Honest Framing & Limitations¶

  • Observational Nature: This analysis is strictly observational and describes statistical associations within the FlyRank dataset. It does not establish causality, nor does it attempt to reverse-engineer or claim insights into proprietary search engine algorithms.
  • Predictive Boundary: As documented in the results, engineered relative temporal features (impression_momentum, position_change, ctr_change) exhibit high statistical noise and do not yield class separation. Therefore, rather than forcing an overfit predictive model, this framework pivots to an objective risk-exposure mitigation tool where pages are ranked by absolute traffic volume to protect the business's highest-leverage assets.

2. Data¶

Which release, which tables, date windows, what you excluded and why. Public-safe.

Dataset Overview¶

This analysis is built upon the public release of the FlyRank ML Internship Dataset. The study evaluates multi-client search and content performance signals distributed across core relational tables.

Relational Infrastructure & Scale¶

  • dim_clients: 104 client metadata rows mapping technical profile availability.
  • dim_content: 519,606 tracked content assets.
  • fact_daily: 78,835,655 rows recording transactional daily SEO performance.
  • fact_query_90d: 2,414,248 query-level behavioral records.

Temporal Windows¶

  • Historical Feature Window: April 1, 2026, to May 31, 2026 ($60\text{ days}$).
  • Target Observation Window: June 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026 ($30\text{ days}$).
  • Total Longitudinal Horizon: 520 unique observation dates (2025-01-27 to 2026-06-30) inside the underlying warehouse.

Public-Safety Exclusions & Guardrails¶

To enforce absolute data privacy and maintain a public-safe research baseline, the following elements are scrubbed or excluded from all layers of analysis:

  1. Identifiable Attributes: Complete omission of real client corporate names, operational domain names, explicit source URLs, and private search queries.
  2. Security Credentials: Hard omission of any access tokens, connection configurations, or platform credentials.
  3. Analytical Boundary: Exclusion of URLs generating fewer than 100 organic impressions during the $60\text{-day}$ historical feature phase to eliminate extreme long-tail keyword volatility and data sparseness.

3. Methodology¶

Assumptions, features, label definition, baseline, validation design, leakage checks.

Label Definition¶

The target variable $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ represents the binary class state is_declining. It is mathematically formulated using the relative percentage change in organic search impressions between the historical base month ($M_{\text{may}}$) and the forward observation target month ($M_{\text{june}}$):

$$\Delta_{\text{impressions}} = \frac{\text{impressions}_{june} - \text{impressions}_{may}}{\text{impressions}_{may}} \times 100$$

$$Y = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \Delta_{\text{impressions}} \le -20 \\ 0 & \text{if } \Delta_{\text{impressions}} > -20 \end{cases}$$

Feature Space Matrix¶

The finalized V2 feature space encompasses $12$ multi-dimensional operational metrics structured across three analytical groups:

  1. Absolute Base Volumetrics: impressions_60d, clicks_60d, avg_position_60d, impressions_apr, impressions_may, clicks_may.
  2. Temporal Momentum Signals: impression_momentum, position_change.
  3. User Engagement/Efficiency Ratios: may_ctr, apr_ctr, ctr_change, avg_position_apr, avg_position_may.

Validation Design & Data Leakage Controls¶

  • Group-Aware Splitting Architecture: Simple random splitting violates independence assumptions in multi-tenant SEO structures because pages from the same client share systemic infrastructure, domain authorities, and market shocks. To guard against out-of-sample data leakage, a strict GroupShuffleSplit was executed.
  • Leakage Separation Guardrail: The splitting mechanism reserves exactly $20\%$ of distinct client hashes strictly for testing. This guarantees that a client's records exist either completely within the training space or completely within the test evaluation space—forcing models to validate on entirely unseen corporate ecosystems.

Naive Baseline Definition¶

The evaluation architecture implements an "Always Decline Baseline" ($Y_{\text{pred}} = 1$). This naive heuristic mirrors the high-priors class unbalance where approximately $\sim 64\%$ of test-split assets experience performance drops. It establishes a critical functional benchmark for precision, recall, and $F_1\text{-score}$ metrics.

4. Results (vs baseline)¶

Model vs baseline on the same split. The honest table.

Model Performance & The "Always-Decline" Baseline¶

To evaluate the predictive power of the V2 feature set, a Random Forest Classifier was trained and tested using a strict client-level GroupShuffleSplit. Its performance was benchmarked against a naive "Always Decline" baseline (predicting $Y=1$ for all assets).

Key Findings:

  1. Failure to Outperform Naive Baseline: The Random Forest achieved an $F_1\text{-score}$ of $0.7678$, which is strictly lower than the naive baseline's $F_1\text{-score}$ of $0.7780$.
  2. Feature Importance Validation: The model heavily over-indexed on absolute volume metrics (avg_position_60d, impressions_apr, impressions_may) while virtually ignoring the engineered temporal momentum signals (ctr_change, position_change).

Because the machine learning model failed to provide predictive lift over a simple deterministic baseline, deploying the ML model introduces unnecessary technical debt and opacity. The results strongly dictate reverting to a transparent, rule-based ranking queue based on absolute traffic volume (Risk Exposure).

5. Limitations¶

What this work cannot claim.

Correlation vs. Causality¶

This analysis is entirely observational and relies on retrospective historical data. Consequently, the relationship between your base signals and June declines is strictly correlative, not causal. For example, while a drop in Click-Through Rate (ctr_change) might co-occur with a traffic decline, this work cannot claim that optimizing meta-descriptions to boost CTR will systematically prevent or reverse an algorithm update or traffic decline.

The Blind Spot of Naive Baseline Sorting¶

By pivoting to a risk-exposure framework sorted strictly by absolute traffic volume (impressions_may), the model introduces a explicit bias toward high-visibility pages. While this protects the business's highest-leverage assets, it introduces two massive functional vulnerabilities:

  1. The Growth Trap (False Positives): A page with massive volume that is rapidly accelerating will be surfaced as a top priority for review, even though its operational trajectory is healthy (as observed in Row 4 of our inspection).
  2. The Quiet Decay (False Negatives): A low-impression page that is experiencing severe, complete indexation drops or technical penalty breaks will be pushed to the bottom of the queue. If a page with 400 impressions drops to 0, our system completely ignores it, masking localized technical issues.

Stationary Assumptions¶

This framework assumes that the structural trends holding true in April/May will persist into June. Search engine algorithmic parameters, competitor entries, and market seasonality are inherently non-stationary; shifts in external macroeconomic conditions will degrade the validity of this ranking system over time.

6. Ranked recommendations¶

The action playbook output — the paper's recommendations section.

Action Playbook: The Risk-Exposure Queue¶

Because the signal audit proved that relative momentum does not reliably separate declining pages from growing pages, we built our ranked action playbook using a Risk Exposure framework.

Assuming the baseline heuristic that the majority of content will decay (as predicted by our naive baseline), we rank the action queue strictly by descending impressions_may. This ensures that the content team's limited review bandwidth is directed at the pages with the most absolute traffic to lose.

Skeptical Inspection of the Top 10¶

A manual review of the top 10 ranked pages demonstrates the pragmatic value of this approach:

  1. High-Value Catches: It successfully flagged 6 massive traffic collapses, including one page dropping from 219,982 to just 6,001 impressions in the forward window.
  2. The Cost of Simplicity (False Positives): It also flagged 4 pages that either stayed flat or grew.

In an applied business context, the cost of a false positive (spending a few minutes reviewing a high-traffic page that is actually healthy) is negligible compared to the cost of missing a massive traffic collapse on a high-value asset. Prioritizing by absolute volume ensures the content team spends their time protecting the most critical pages.
